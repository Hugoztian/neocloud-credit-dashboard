This is the Capacity and supply tab. It shows backlogs and remaining performance obligations, installed versus planned power, and supplier support from Nvidia, A-M-D, Google and others. Definitions differ, so the figures are not strictly comparable. Oracle reports 664 billion dollars of remaining performance obligations and is shown in the table only, so that it does not dwarf the chart. CoreWeave's backlog is about 132.5 billion, N-scale's total contract value about 105.8 billion, and SpaceX 63 billion. Across the tracked public and I-P-O-pending names, excluding Oracle, backlog sums to about 421 billion dollars.

Where megawatts are disclosed, planned or contracted capacity is a multiple of what is already installed. Grid risk is rising: around 191 gigawatts of planned data-centre capacity faces new uncertainty as the build-out slows, according to mid-November reporting.

Supplier support is narrowing. Nvidia has cut its neocloud commitments to 32 billion dollars, from 36 billion, and they only take effect once financing is secured and capacity is deployed. Total Nvidia guarantees are 118 billion, including up to 105 billion of residual-value support for OpenAI's Ohio campus. Google backstops appear at Cipher, TeraWulf, Hut 8 and Fluidstack sites. Our view: contracted demand looks large, but much of it is conditional, and the backstops are moving off the balance sheet. This is not investment advice.
